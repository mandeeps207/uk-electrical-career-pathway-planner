Work out what to investigate next, wherever you are in your electrical training or career change.

Electrical training and occupational competence are not the same thing. A Level 2 or Level 3 technical qualification can form part of your progression, but classroom study alone does not automatically demonstrate occupational competence to work unsupervised as an electrician.

Your exact recognised pathway and eligibility should always be checked against current ECS, NET, awarding-body and training-provider guidance.

      02 / GUIDANCE
      01

      Technical Training vs Occupational Competence

      Technical courses teach knowledge and practical techniques. Occupational competence is evidenced through the recognised requirements of a specific route, often including assessed work in real settings. A course title alone does not settle this.

      02

      What Happens After Level 2?

      Look at Level 3 training and how you could gain supervised electrical experience. Ask a provider exactly how the proposed course connects to an industry-recognised competency route, including any workplace evidence required.

      03

      What Happens After Level 3?

      Check the full name and units of your qualification. If it is a technical certificate, ask what additional workplace assessment or recognised programme your intended occupation requires. Do not assume Level 3 study alone permits unsupervised work.

      04

      Getting Electrical Workplace Experience

      Ask employers and providers what supervised tasks you could do, who would oversee them, and whether the work would offer evidence relevant to your chosen qualification. Record duties and dates, then check evidence requirements with your assessor.

      05

      Electrician's Mate and Electrical Labourer Roles

      Employer titles such as Electrician's Mate, Electrical Improver and Trainee Electrician vary between employers; they are not universal qualification categories. The ECS Electrical Labourer card describes a supervised support role. A job title alone does not prove that work meets NVQ evidence rules.

      06

      NVQ / Recognised Competency Qualifications

      Competency qualifications assess the knowledge and practical performance required by their specific standard. Before enrolling, check the qualification code, entry conditions, required work evidence, assessment arrangements and how it relates to your intended occupation.

      07

      Where AM2 Fits

      NET delivers different assessments for different routes, including AM2, AM2S and AM2E. Ask your provider which assessment applies to your programme and when you should take it. Use NET's candidate guidance and readiness material before booking.

      08

      Understanding ECS Progression

      ECS cards reflect specific occupations and qualification requirements. Apprentice, trainee, labourer and electrician cards have different criteria. Check the current ECS requirements for the exact card you have in mind; this planner cannot decide eligibility.
